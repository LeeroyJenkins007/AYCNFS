Here's the full set of equations you need for a numerical ascent simulation, with Kerbin-specific constants.

## Kerbin Constants

| Symbol | Value |
|---|---|
| R (radius) | 600,000 m |
| μ (GM) | 3.5316 × 10¹² m³/s² |
| g₀ (surface gravity) | 9.81 m/s² |
| ρ₀ (sea-level density) | 1.225 kg/m³ |
| p₀ (sea-level pressure) | 101,325 Pa |
| H (scale height, simple) | 5,000 m |

## Atmospheric Model
DONE

## Equations of Motion (Flight-Path Frame)

You integrate these numerically (RK4 or even Euler-Cromer with small dt):

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{T}{m} - \frac{D}{m} - g(h)\,\sin\gamma$$

$$\frac{d\gamma}{dt} = -\frac{g(h)}{v}\cos\gamma + \frac{v}{R+h}\cos\gamma \quad \text{(gravity turn)}$$

$$\frac{dh}{dt} = v \sin\gamma$$

$$\frac{dm}{dt} = -\dot{m} \quad \text{(while engine is on)}$$

where γ is the **flight path angle from horizontal** (γ = 90° at liftoff, γ → 0° at orbit).

## Force Terms

**Thrust:**
$$T = \dot{m} \cdot v_e = \dot{m} \cdot I_{sp} \cdot g_0$$

**Drag:**
$$D = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho(h)\,v^2\,C_D\,A$$

- C_D ≈ 0.3–0.5 for a typical KSP rocket (nose cone helps)
- A = max cross-sectional area (m²)

**Gravity:**
$$g(h) = \frac{\mu}{(R+h)^2}$$

## Extracting Losses from the Simulation

Once you have the full trajectory, the losses are:

$$\Delta v_{\text{grav}} = \int_0^{t_b} g(h)\,\sin\gamma \; dt$$

$$\Delta v_{\text{drag}} = \int_0^{t_b} \frac{D(t)}{m(t)} \; dt$$

And the total velocity gained:

$$\Delta v_{\text{total}} = v_{\text{final}} = I_{sp}\,g_0\,\ln\frac{m_0}{m_f} - \Delta v_{\text{grav}} - \Delta v_{\text{drag}}$$

## Simulation Pseudocode

```
dt = 0.1 s  (or smaller for accuracy)
h = 0; v = 0; γ = π/2; m = m_wet

for t in 0..t_burn:
    g  = μ / (R + h)²
    ρ  = ρ₀ * exp(-h / 5000)          # or USSA lookup
    D  = 0.5 * ρ * v² * Cd * A
    T  = mdot * Isp * g0              # 0 if engine off
    dm/dt = -mdot                      # 0 if engine off

    dv  = (T - D)/m - g*sin(γ)
    dγ  = -(g/v)*cos(γ) + (v/(R+h))*cos(γ)
    dh  = v * sin(γ)

    v += dv * dt
    γ += dγ * dt
    h += dh * dt
    m += (dm/dt) * dt

    # accumulate losses
    v_grav_loss += g * sin(γ) * dt
    v_drag_loss += (D/m) * dt
```

## Practical Notes for KSP

- **KSP's drag model** actually uses a more complex formula that includes a Mach-dependent drag coefficient and a "lift" term for asymmetric shapes. The simple ½ρv²C_D·A is a good first approximation.
- **Max-q** (peak dynamic pressure q = ½ρv²) typically hits around 8–12 km altitude in KSP. Your simulation will show this naturally.
- **Gravity turn rate**: In KSP, the standard "pitch to 45° at 1 km, 90° at 70 km" style turn is roughly what the gravity turn equation produces for TWR ~1.5. You can also just fix γ as a function of altitude (e.g., γ = 90° - 45°·(h/70000)) to mimic a manual turn profile.
- **Orbit insertion**: Once you're above ~70 km (ρ ≈ 0), you just need to burn prograde until v = √(μ/(R+h_target)) for a circular orbit. That final burn is pure Tsiolkovsky with no losses.

This gives you a fully self-contained simulator — no delta-v maps needed. You just feed in your rocket's mass, Isp, thrust, C_D, and A, and it tells you exactly where you end up.
